Graduation project · 2018

A web application for managing clientele information at OCS-Consulting

Contracts lived in Word, everything around them lived in spreadsheets, and every consultant kept their assignments their own way. The project replaced that with one place to record an assignment, and asked whether the data was worth reading once it was there.

  • ClientOCS-Consulting B.V.
  • Where's-Hertogenbosch, Netherlands
  • Year2018
  • RoleGraduation project, built and documented solo

What the firm does

OCS-Consulting supplies people and services to other businesses: consultants placed on site, consultancy work, and infrastructure support, for corporations and smaller firms alike. Its own expertise sits in project management, business analysis, business intelligence, and testing and development on SAS software.

Every piece of that work is a clientele assignment, and every assignment rests on a contract. The contract holds who the client is and what was agreed. Everything around it lived somewhere else: how long the work runs, the number it carries, which consultants are on it.

The contracts were written in Word. The detail around them sat in spreadsheets. The management reports meant to give a view across every assignment at once were built from those same spreadsheets. In 2018 the firm decided to stop treating this as a habit and look at it as a project.

Where it was breaking

Three things were going wrong, and only the first was obvious.

Handling assignments by hand meant paperwork and spreadsheets, which is tedious and easy to get wrong. That is tolerable at one size and not at another, and the firm was growing.

There was no standard way of doing it, so each consultant worked out their own. That holds up until somebody else has to pick the work up.

And because the documents varied, so did the data inside them. Quality fell, and with it the chance of finding anything useful when someone went back to look.

What the project set out to do

The brief was a data-driven web application: one place to record a clientele assignment, and the same way of recording it every time, so that the process was standardised rather than invented afresh by whoever happened to be doing it.

Underneath that sat a second question, and it is the more interesting of the two.

Can the process be standardised?

Record every clientele assignment one way, so a reference made later finds what it is looking for.

Is the data worth reading?

The firm also wanted to know whether the same data, once collected, held anything useful when put through a business intelligence tool.

How the project ran

It was run as a project in its own right rather than a build with notes attached. The survey came first, then a plan carrying its own communication plan and risk analysis, then requirements set against MoSCoW before anything was written. Six meetings were minuted, and the process itself was recorded against the DOT framework.

Built with

Four things. The database carried the model, the PHP layer over it was generated from that schema with AppGini, the interface was plain HTML and CSS, and Power BI Desktop was where the second question got its answer.

  • HTML & CSS
  • MySQL
  • PHP, generated with AppGini
  • Microsoft Power BI Desktop
